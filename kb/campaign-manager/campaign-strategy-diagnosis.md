# Campaign Strategy Diagnosis

## Diagnosis
**What type of campaign should I run?**

The wrong campaign strategy leaves money on the table. Most teams default to broadcast campaigns (send to everyone) because they're simple to execute. But broadcast campaigns treat all customers equally, which means high-value segments get the same message as low-intent prospects. The result: diluted ROI, message fatigue, and wasted budget.

Your campaign type should depend on three factors:
1. Your business objective (awareness, engagement, conversion, retention)
2. Your customer data maturity (can you segment reliably?)
3. Your execution capacity (can your stack handle orchestration?)

## Context

### Campaign Types by Objective

**Awareness Campaigns**
- Goal: Reach new audiences, build brand recall
- Best for: Product launches, market expansion, seasonal promotions
- Typical approach: Broadcast with reach optimization
- Example: "New feature available" to all inactive users

**Engagement Campaigns**
- Goal: Increase interaction, reduce churn, deepen relationships
- Best for: Lifecycle retention, feature adoption, community building
- Typical approach: Segmented by engagement tier + behavior
- Example: Send tutorials to users who haven't completed onboarding

**Conversion Campaigns**
- Goal: Drive immediate purchase or action
- Best for: Sales promotions, time-sensitive offers, high-intent audiences
- Typical approach: Triggered by behavior + sequential follow-up
- Example: "Complete checkout" reminder 2 hours after cart abandonment

**Retention Campaigns**
- Goal: Prevent churn, maximize customer lifetime value
- Best for: Subscription businesses, recurring revenue models
- Typical approach: Rule-based lifecycle campaigns
- Example: Win-back campaign for churned users who haven't logged in 60+ days

### Execution Models

**Broadcast Campaigns**
- Send to entire database or broad segment
- Lowest complexity, fastest execution
- Highest unsubscribe risk, lowest personalization
- ROI: 2-4x (depending on offer quality)

**Segmented Campaigns**
- Divide audience by criteria (geography, behavior, value), tailor messaging
- Medium complexity, requires audience data
- Better conversion, lower unsubscribe rate
- ROI: 5-8x (vs. broadcast baseline)

**Triggered Campaigns**
- Automated execution based on user actions (visit, purchase, cart abandon)
- High complexity, requires event tracking + automation
- Highest relevance, best timing, lowest fatigue
- ROI: 8-15x (highest efficiency)

**Sequential Campaigns**
- Multi-touch journey: email → SMS → push → retargeting ad
- Very high complexity, requires channel orchestration
- Best for high-intent audiences, proven conversion uplift
- ROI: 10-20x (for conversion campaigns)

## Recommended Approach

**Start with: Segmented + Triggered**

This combination balances simplicity and ROI:
- **Segmented**: Divide your audience into 3-5 coherent groups (high-value, mid-value, low-value / active, dormant / recently-purchased, longtime-customer)
- **Triggered**: Automate campaigns based on behavior (purchase, cart abandon, feature adoption milestone)
- **Benefit**: 6-10x ROI improvement over broadcast without requiring complex orchestration
- **Effort**: 2-3 weeks to set up, 30 minutes per campaign to execute

**When to evolve to Sequential:**
- Once segmented + triggered is running smoothly (2+ campaigns)
- When you have clear high-intent audiences (cart abandoners, trial sign-ups)
- When multi-channel reach is critical to your business
- Investment: 2-3x effort, 3-5x ROI potential

## Follow-Up Questions

1. **Primary Objective**: Are you optimizing for awareness, engagement, conversion, or retention? (This determines everything else)
2. **Audience Data**: Can you reliably segment your audience today? (If not, we need to build that first)
3. **Execution Capacity**: Do you have event tracking and automation set up? (Or do we start with simpler rules?)
4. **Channel Mix**: Are you email-only, or do you want to orchestrate across email + SMS + push? (Scope determines complexity)
