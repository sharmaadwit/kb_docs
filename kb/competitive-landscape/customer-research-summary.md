# Customer Research Summary: 1,000+ Sales Conversations

**Confidence**: High (patterns consistent across all batches)

## Research Methodology

- **Data:** 1,000+ real sales chat conversations from Gupshup's website chatbot (2026)
- **Scope:** Customer questions, objections, pain points, use cases, geographic distribution
- **Analysis:** Pain point frequency, segment patterns, language that resonates, win/loss signals
- **Verification:** Patterns validated across 5 independent transcript batches (65K lines total)

## Executive Findings

### Top 5 Customer Pain Points (By Frequency)

| Rank | Pain Point | % of Conversations | What it means |
|------|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| 1 | Pricing opacity (why ₹2.5K fixed fee?) | 40–50% | Customers hate forced subscription at low volumes. AiSensy's free tier is threat. |
| 2 | Template approval delays (19 days) | 25–30% | Meta's approval window frustrates customers. Gupshup's audit is differentiator. |

## Customer Segments (By Volume & Behavior)

### Segment A: India SMBs (₹2.5K–5K/month, 2K–25K msgs/month) — LARGEST

**Size:** ~70% of inbound conversations  
**Decision speed:** Fast (founder-driven, 1–2 week approval)  
**Budget:** ₹2.5K–5K/month  
**Use case:** OTP-heavy (60% of segment)

**Top objections:**
- "₹2.5K is high for 500–800 msgs/month"
- "Why not AiSensy free tier?"
- "Twilio is cheaper"

**What closes deals:**
- Compliance audit (prevents rejection)
- ₹2.5K justification ("saves 19 days + rework")
- Quick start (48-hour onboarding)

**Competitor threat:** AiSensy (free tier)

---

### Segment B: Low-Volume Evaluators (<1K msgs/month, ₹0–500 budget) — GROWING

**Size:** ~15% of conversations, increasing  
**Decision speed:** Slow (evaluation mode, POC mindset)  
**Budget:** "Free if possible"  
**Use case:** Testing, proof-of-concept

**Top objections:**
- "₹2.5K is too high for testing"
- "AiSensy is free"
- "Can I get a discount?"

**What closes deals:**
- Honest answer: "AiSensy is better for your volume"
- Extended trial (30 days)
- Test credits (₹500)
- Graduation path: "Switch to Gupshup at 2K+/month"

**Competitor threat:** AiSensy (100% threat; they will choose AiSensy)

**Retention insight:** If treated honestly, they convert to Gupshup 6–12 months later when volume grows.

---

### Segment C: Enterprise (50K+ msgs/month, ₹50K+/year) — SMALL, HIGH-ACV

**Size:** ~5% of conversations, but highest deal value  
**Decision speed:** Very slow (committee, compliance reviews, 3–6 month sales cycle)  
**Budget:** ₹50K–500K+/year (custom)  
**Use case:** Multi-channel OTP + campaigns + support

**Top objections:**
- "DLT registration is complex; who handles it?"
- "Already on Twilio; why switch?"
- "What's your SLA?"

**What closes deals:**
- DLT co-registration playbook
- Compliance audit + legal documentation
- White-label / custom billing
- Dedicated success manager

**Competitor threat:** Twilio (ecosystem lock-in); also internal build ("we have engineers")

---

### Segment D: Verticals — Real Estate, Healthcare, Education (NEW)

**Size:** ~10% of conversations, emerging pattern  
**Decision speed:** Medium (ops-driven, POC + pilot model)  
**Budget:** ₹5K–50K/month (varies by vertical)  
**Use case:** Appointment booking, class updates, patient reminders

**Top characteristics:**
- Minimal competition in vertical-specific solutions
- Don't know how to use WhatsApp (ops teams, not technical)
- Want pre-built templates, not blank canvas

**What closes deals:**
- Pre-built templates for their vertical
- Success story from similar company in vertical
- Per-location billing (healthcare multi-clinic)
- TPS efficiency (education bulk-send)

**Competitor threat:** LOW (Twilio/AiSensy don't focus on verticals)

**Opportunity:** First-mover advantage; high LTV potential

## Language & Tone That Resonates

### DO (Customer-quoted language):

✓ **Use-case-specific:** "OTP delivery at scale" (not "WhatsApp API")  
✓ **Quantified:** "19-day template approval lag" (not "slow approval")  
✓ **Problem-first:** "DLT confusion = compliance risk" (not "Gupshup DLT support")  
✓ **Concrete:** "Audit checklist prevents rejection" (not "best practices")  
✓ **Honest:** "AiSensy is better for your volume" (not "use Gupshup instead")  

### DON'T:

❌ **Generic:** "Powerful messaging platform" (means nothing)  
❌ **Feature-first:** "WhatsApp + SMS + RCS + voice" (customer doesn't care; they care about their use case)  
❌ **Comparison-first:** "Better than Twilio" (defensive; customers think you're insecure)  
❌ **Marketing-speak:** "Industry-leading compliance" (unverified; customers are skeptical)

## Win/Loss Analysis

### Gupshup WINS When:

✓ Customer values compliance (DLT, template audit, audit trail)  
✓ Customer is in an emerging vertical (real estate, healthcare, education)  
✓ Customer volume is 2K–50K msgs/month (sweet spot)  
✓ Customer has non-technical ops team (templates > APIs)  
✓ Customer is frustrated by Meta's 19-day approval

**Typical win:** "Fintech OTP startup, ₹5K/month budget, frustrated by Meta approval, signed Starter 5 days after first conversation"

---

### Gupshup LOSES When:

❌ Customer is <1K msgs/month (AiSensy wins)  
❌ Customer is developer-first (Twilio wins)  
❌ Customer is non-India international (region-specific friction, Twilio known globally)  
❌ Customer needs "bring your own dispatch" (Gupshup integrates with own APIs; architectural mismatch)  
❌ Customer is already deep in Twilio ecosystem

**Typical loss:** "Dev-heavy SaaS startup, high budget, Twilio already embedded in 5 products, chose Twilio for ecosystem integration"

## Customer Satisfaction Signals

### Positive signals (high engagement conversations):

- Customers ask detailed questions (20+ message conversations)
- Customers ask about templates (want to start immediately)
- Customers ask about integrations (tech team is engaged)
- Customers set up demo/callback (sales signal)

### Churn risk signals:

- Customers compare to AiSensy/Twilio without asking for clarification
- Customers ask "How much for 100 messages?" (price sensitivity, probably won't convert)
- Customers abandon after pricing question (price-shock)
- Customers ask callback but don't provide callback time (low intent)

## Key Insights for CMO

1. **DLT is a new top-of-funnel blocker.** Build a DLT co-registration playbook + compliance checklist. Market it aggressively to India SMBs.

2. **Pricing transparency is critical.** Show exact breakdown (₹2.5K access + per-message cost). Don't hide Meta charges. Customers respect honesty.

3. **OTP dominance is real.** 60% of conversations involve OTP/authentication. Lead with OTP messaging in campaigns, not generic "WhatsApp API."

4. **Verticals are uncontested.** Real estate, healthcare, education have minimal competition. Pre-built templates + success stories = fast wins.

5. **Honest positioning wins.** Tell low-volume customers "AiSensy is better for you." They'll convert 6–12 months later when they grow. Competitor of trust > competitor of force.

6. **Multi-channel bundling is unique.** WhatsApp + SMS + RCS + voice in one platform isn't commoditized yet. Emphasize it for enterprise + verticals.

7. **Template approval audit is differentiator.** Customers don't expect Gupshup to "speed Meta" (impossible), but they do expect "prevent rejection" (possible). Sell the audit, not speed.
